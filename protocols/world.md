# Stage 2: Getting to Know Your World

Read after the first-date discovery when the user wants greater relevance. This is optional and progressive, not a compulsory intake interview.

## Purpose

Learn the *situational* context that makes advice accurate: responsibilities, active efforts, decision history, relationships, prior attempts, commitments, and constraints. Discover only what helps with the user's stated goals. A person's world is not a dossier.

## Rules

- Ask permission before starting: "Would it help if we mapped the parts of your world that come up most often? We can keep it focused and private."
- Ask **one question at a time**. Start with the domains most relevant to current work. Do not solicit names or personal details without need.
- Separate stable role descriptions, active matters, and history. Record minimal relational information, not judgments about third parties.
- Ask permission before recording third-party details and before using them in broader project/shared contexts. Avoid sensitive information by default.
- If the user declines, continue the original task without penalty. Never require all domains.

## Guided discovery

### W1. Roles and competing responsibilities
**Anchor:** "What are the different hats you wear that most often influence the decisions you ask an AI to help with?"
**Probe:** "Where do those responsibilities compete for time, attention, or priorities?"
**Capture:** User-chosen role map, tensions, and scope. Do not assume work or family structure.

### W2. Active initiatives and goals
**Anchor:** "What are the two or three things you're actively trying to move forward right now?"
**Probe:** "What would progress look like, and what is currently blocking it?"
**Capture:** Initiative, goal, current state, constraints, review date.

### W3. Relevant relationships and collaboration dynamics
**Anchor:** "Are there recurring collaborators or groups whose roles I should understand when helping you communicate or decide?"
**Probe:** "Without sharing anything private you don't want saved, how does that working relationship affect the choices you make?"
**Capture:** Minimal user-approved relationship role, collaboration pattern, context-specific boundaries. Do **not** store allegations or psychological judgments as facts; distinguish the user's account from independently confirmed facts.

### W4. History of decisions, attempts, and reasons
**Anchor:** "Is there a situation you return to often where it would help if I remembered what you've already tried and why?"
**Probe:** "What did you decide, what changed, and what should I avoid suggesting again without new information?"
**Capture:** Dated decisions, rationale, outcomes, superseded options, open questions. Link to initiative; avoid copying full transcripts.

### W5. Practical constraints and the broader picture
**Anchor:** "What constraints would make an otherwise good recommendation unrealistic for you?"
**Probe:** "Which constraints are enduring, and which are just true for now?"
**Capture:** Time, resources, accessibility, schedule, obligations, dependencies; respect sensitive boundaries.

### W6. Current mode and support needs
**Anchor:** "When you bring something difficult or unfinished, how can I tell whether you want me to listen, explore, challenge, or move into action?"
**Probe:** "What signals should I notice, and when should I simply ask rather than assume?"
**Capture:** Collaboration cues only. Do not store momentary emotions as personality traits or infer mental health.

### W7. Formative experiences (optional)
**Anchor:** "Are there experiences that shaped your approach to decisions or working with others, which you'd *want* an assistant to keep in mind?"
**Probe:** "What's the lesson you took from that experience, without details you'd rather keep private?"
**Capture:** User-approved lesson, not necessarily the story. Offer session-only handling.

## Situation summary

After relevant domains, propose a **World Map** using `templates/world-context.md`:

- Stable roles and constraints (user-selected)
- Active initiatives, each with last-reviewed date
- Minimal relevant collaborators and boundaries
- Decision history and attempts, tied to initiatives
- Unknowns and items to revisit
- Items explicitly excluded or session-only

Ask which entries belong at global, private project, or session scope. **Do not create a universal dossier.** Offer omission and redaction before persisting. For public/shared project context, use only approved, non-sensitive summaries.

## Just-in-time mode

During later work, if a recommendation depends on missing history, ask **one targeted question** such as: "Before I suggest next steps, what have you already tried?" Do not restart the interview. Continue with qualified advice if the user skips.
